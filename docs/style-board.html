Six ways to light an empty office

The cars are toon-shaded with ink outlines. The office they drive through is physically shaded with an ACES film curve. That's two art directions in one frame — and nothing in the render tells you the cars are 18 cm long. Below: the same corner of the open-plan floor, drawn six ways, so the choice can be made by looking rather than by arguing.

Two languages

Toy cars visiting a semi-real office. Every direction here is really a proposal for which side wins.

No scale cue

Scale is asserted by geometry — a mug is car-height — and never by the camera. fov: 60, deep focus, no macro read.

House-style post

N8AO + bloom + ACES is what a good R3F scene looks like by default. The curve also eats the amber the UI is built on.

Zero assets

Every texture is a 128px canvas. Directions that live in shading and palette are affordable; craft looks are not.

First pass — what survives the constraints

Drag across any frame to wipe back to today's build

Everything above was picked by asking what won’t break? — twelve cars at 60 fps, zero external assets, readability as a mechanic. That filter is honest and it is also how you end up with a competent look nobody recognises. So: the same question asked the other way round — what would make a screenshot of this game unmistakable? Two of these are house styles. Three are layers that sit on top of whichever house style wins.

Second pass — what would be unmistakable

Where this lands

Tabletop After Dark is the safe pick — it fixes the missing scale cue, agrees with the shipped UI, and it's camera, light and post with no material rewrite. Copier Room costs about the same (one fullscreen pass, no material knowledge at all) at much higher variance, and it's the only entry here that would make someone repost a screenshot. On Twos is close to free and composes with either, so it isn't really competing — if only one thing gets built, there's an argument it should be that.
